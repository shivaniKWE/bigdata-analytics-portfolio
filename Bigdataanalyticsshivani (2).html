Collection of Dataset

The IMDB Dataset, comprising 50K Movie Reviews, contains a total of 50,000 critiques of films (each labeled as either positive or negative sentiments). Notably, there is an equal division: 25,000 reviews for each class.

Business Problem

The research was undertaken to illuminate how audiences interpret various films cataloged on IMDB, with a particular emphasis on analyzing reviews associated with these films. The primary objective was to employ machine learning and natural language processing (NLP) techniques to automatically categorize movie reviews into two overarching categories: positive sentiment and negative sentiment. This classification is essential, because it facilitates the accomplishment of multiple business objectives, such as recommending similar films according to audience preferences, marketing movies through targeted advertisements for those that have performed well in audience ratings and assisting movie studios in pinpointing elements that lead to audience dissatisfaction. This information is critical (however), as it enables studios to produce more content that resonates with viewers. Although the primary aim is to deepen the understanding of audience sentiment, it is anticipated that this work will ultimately enhance the customer experience by improving the overall quality of entertainment services they receive.

This process tokenizes and lemmatizes the text, which means it reduces words to their base forms; however, it also eliminates common English stop words. def preprocess_text(text):
    text = re.sub(r'<.*?>', '', text)
    text = re.sub(r'http\S+|www\S+|https\S+', '', text, flags=re.MULTILINE)
    text = re.sub(r'[^\x00-\x7F]+', '', text)
    text = text.lower()
    text = re.sub(r'[\d\W]+', ' ', text)
    return text
def tokenize_and_lemmatize(text):
    sentences = sent_tokenize(text)
    processed_sentences = []

    for sentence in sentences:
        words = word_tokenize(sentence)
        lemmatized_words = [lemmatizer.lemmatize(word) for word in words if word.lower() not in stop_words]
        processed_sentences.append(' '.join(lemmatized_words))
    return ' '.join(processed_sentences)

Feature extraction involves generating numerical representations of textual data through two primary methodologies: Bag-of-Words (BoW), which counts the occurrences of words within each document and TF-IDF, which weighs the significance of words according to their frequency in a specific document and across the entire corpus. However, these methods impose a limitation on vocabulary size, capping it at the 5000 most frequently used words. This is important because it ensures manageability and efficiency in processing the data, although it may exclude some less common terms that could be relevant.

Transformers

The Transformer model represents a sophisticated neural network architecture that is fundamentally based on self-attention mechanisms which process sequential data, such as text and audio. Although RNNs (Recurrent Neural Networks) handle data in a sequential manner, this particular structure enables the parallelization of training, thus significantly accelerating the process. The essence of learning within the Transformer lies in the encoder-decoder architecture of any given type, which effectively translates the input features extracted by the encoder into the corresponding output sequence via a decoder. The attention mechanism available facilitates the model's ability to concentrate on various segments of the input sequence when generating the output; this capability enables it to capture long-range dependencies and intricate relationships within the data. These advancements have rendered the Transformer remarkably effective for tasks such as machine translation, text summarization and question answering. However, its complexity may present challenges in certain applications.
